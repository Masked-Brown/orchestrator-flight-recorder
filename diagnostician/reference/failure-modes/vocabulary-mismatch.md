# Vocabulary mismatch

**In one line.** An answer delivered in words the reader cannot evaluate, so the check that
would have caught the problem never happens.

## What it is

The content may be entirely correct. The failure is in the **register**: the reply is written in
terms the person reading it has no way to assess — internal names, abbreviations, terms invented
earlier in the same session and now used as though long established, or a description of the
system that requires the system's own vocabulary to follow.

The reader is then stuck. They cannot approve it on the merits and they cannot reject it on the
merits, because they cannot form a judgment about it at all. What they do instead is one of two
things, and both are damaging:

- **They wave it through.** Review stops being a filter. Whatever is wrong now travels
  unchallenged, and the report that should have caught it is the thing that hid it.
- **They stall.** The conversation circles the same ground, because the person cannot say yes
  and cannot say what is wrong.

The damage is always downstream of the message that commits the mode. This one rarely surfaces
where it starts.

## What it looks like in the record

- **Terms with no definition anywhere in the window**, or defined only in terms that are
  themselves undefined here.
- **Vocabulary coined mid-session and immediately treated as established** — a name for a
  component, a stage, a pattern, used from its second appearance as though it needs no gloss.
- **The reply after it is short and procedural.** "Ok." "Go on." "Fine." No engagement with any
  specific claim. Treat this as a signal, not proof: brief replies are also just how people talk.
  What makes it evidence is the *combination* — a dense turn, followed by acknowledgement with no
  content, followed by the problem continuing.
- **Or the opposite tell:** a request to say it again, a question that shows the previous turn
  did not land, or a visible pause in the work.
- **Repetition without progress.** The same subject returns two or three times, and nothing gets
  decided about it.
- **In the reasoning channel, where you have it:** the model working on completeness or precision,
  with nothing at all about who is going to read this.

## Telling it apart

| Easily confused with | The question that separates them |
|---|---|
| `ambiguous-instruction` | Who could not read whom? The model could not read the person → ambiguous instruction. The person could not evaluate the model → vocabulary mismatch. |
| `unverified-claim-accepted` | Could the claim have been checked **as written**? If the register made it uncheckable → vocabulary mismatch. If it was perfectly checkable and simply was not checked → unverified claim. |
| `thread-overload` | Was there too **much**, or was it in the wrong **words**? A short, dense, unreadable turn is vocabulary. A stream of individually readable turns nobody can keep up with is overload. |
| `missing-context` | The reader lacking a definition is vocabulary; the **model** lacking a fact is missing context. Mind the direction. |

## What rules it out

- **The reader engaged on the merits.** If their next turn argues with a specific claim, corrects
  a detail, or asks a question that could only be asked by someone who followed it, they could
  evaluate it. Whatever went wrong, it was not this.
- **The terms are defined in the window.** Jargon that has been explained is just vocabulary, and
  a reader who has the definitions is not blocked.
- **The problem would have survived a plain-English version.** Rewrite the turn in your head in
  ordinary words. If the failure still happens, the register was not load-bearing.

## A note for your own report

This is the one mode you are most likely to commit while diagnosing it. A finding written in
words the reporter cannot check is a finding you have not really delivered — and it fails in
exactly the way you are describing. Say what happened in ordinary words, define anything
unavoidable once, in passing, and assume the reader has never seen this system before.
