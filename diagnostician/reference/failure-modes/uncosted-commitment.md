# Uncosted commitment

**In one line.** Work was agreed in full and set running without anyone stating what it would
cost to execute, so the party who set the budget had nothing to weigh it against.

## What it is

The deliverables are settled. Nothing is ambiguous, nothing is missing, nothing has gone out of
date, nobody has asserted anything false. What was never produced is an **estimate** — of
elapsed time, of compute, of money, of anyone's attention — for work whose size only one party
was in a position to see.

The other party is then asked to approve a list of items rather than a bill. A list reads as a
plan: each entry sounds small on its own, and the register that makes an item legible is not
the register that makes it priceable. So approval is given on the contents, and the cost turns
up afterwards as a surprise.

The signature is that the complaint is about the price and not the goods. The deliverables
usually survive review intact. The person is not saying the wrong thing was built; they are
saying they would have chosen differently had they known what it took.

The mode only exists where a cost constraint was actually in force. Someone said they were
short of time, or tired, or wanted this cheap — and that statement is on the recorder. Without
it, an unstated cost is merely an unremarked one, and nothing was breached.

## What it looks like in the record

- **A constraint on cost or pace, stated in adjectives.** Simple, quick, cheap, minimal, nothing
  heavy, done soon. Usually with a reason attached — fatigue, a deadline, a budget — and often
  acknowledged by the other party in its own words, which is useful to you, because a
  restatement shows the constraint was received.
- **A commitment that enumerates deliverables and never prices them.** A plan, a work order, a
  task list. Count the items, then look for the number that says what running them costs. In
  this mode it is not there.
- **Figures present for everything except the thing that matters.** A ten-minute review, a
  two-minute check, an hour of somebody's attention: the cheap and legible parts get priced
  while the expensive part goes unpriced. The sharpest form is a figure quoted for one
  component sitting exactly where a total would sit.
- **The approving party's next act is the execution.** They do not answer the questions the
  commitment contains; they fire it. That silence is the mode working, not a party being idle.
- **The surface is a bill.** An elapsed time, a spend, a token count, reported back with
  surprise — and usually with the deliverables accepted in the same breath.
- **In the reasoning channel, where you have it:** the work being assembled, sequenced and
  justified, with nothing at all about what it will take to run.

## The constraint and the silence both have to be shown

Two anchors are the minimum, and neither substitutes for the other:

1. **The cost constraint in force** — quoted, from a message before the commitment.
2. **The absence of a cost for the committed work** — the exact message range and every channel
   you searched, and the message, quoted, at which a party had to proceed without it.

A finding that shows only the silence is describing ordinary practice. A finding that shows
only the constraint has not yet said anything went wrong.

## Telling it apart

| Easily confused with | The question that separates them |
|---|---|
| `scope-injection` | Line up the deliverables at the surface against the deliverables agreed. A different list → scope. The same list at an unexpected price → this. |
| `unverified-claim-accepted` | Was an estimate **given and wrong**, or **never given**? A bad estimate is a claim that went unchecked. No estimate is this. |
| `vocabulary-mismatch` | Rewrite the commitment in plain words. If the reader still could not have known the cost, the register was not what blocked them. |
| `missing-context` | Mind the direction. A fact the **model** needed and never received is missing context. An estimate only the model could produce and never produced is this. |
| `thread-overload` | Did items pile up unresolved, or were they all delivered at a price nobody named? Delivered → this. |
| `premature-parallelism` | Was the fault in the **order** work was started in, or in the **cost** of work nobody stated? |

## What rules it out

- **A cost was stated for the work as committed.** If any message gives a figure covering what
  was actually fired, this is not the mode — even if the figure was badly wrong, which is a
  different failure with a different origin. A figure covering one component is not a cost for
  the whole; where such a figure is the only one present, that is this mode's sharpest form and
  not a defence against it.
- **The cost was raised.** If the record shows anyone asking what it would take, and an answer
  given or refused, the commitment was priced knowingly or priced badly. Quote that exchange and
  investigate it instead.
- **Nobody could have estimated it.** The mode requires that one party was in a position to
  price the work: a comparable job on the recorder, a costing supplied with the material, its
  own arithmetic. If the cost was genuinely unknowable inside the session, proceeding without it
  was a reasonable act. The estimability is part of the claim and has to be shown, not assumed.
- **No cost constraint was in force.** Search for it, in every channel, and say where you
  searched. If nobody expressed a budget, a pace or a limit, an unremarked cost is not a fault.
- **The complaint is about the deliverables.** If the work itself is disputed, you are looking
  at scope or quality, whatever the bill said.

## A note on the verdict

The verdict turns on who could see the size. Where the party issuing the commitment held the
item list, a comparable costing, or both, and the other party held only adjectives, the mode
sits with whoever wrote the commitment — `mechanical` if that was the model, `pilot-error` if
it was the person. Where a numeric budget had been stated and was exceeded anyway, look again:
that is a departure from a determinate instruction rather than an unpriced one. Where the bill
was inflated by something neither party controlled, the environment is a contributing factor
and rarely the primary cause — check whether the commitment was already unpriced before the
tooling misbehaved.
