# Scope injection

**In one line.** The job quietly got bigger or smaller, and the change was never put on the table
as a change.

## What it is

Work changes shape all the time, and legitimately. This mode is not about the change — it is
about the change **never being surfaced as a decision**, so nobody ever agreed to it and nobody
can point to when it happened.

It runs in both directions and comes from both parties:

- A model adds work nobody asked for — an extra file, an extra feature, an extra section,
  another step — because it looks like the helpful thing to do.
- A model drops something that was asked for, usually by quietly reinterpreting it as covered by
  something else.
- A person adds a requirement mid-flight, phrased as though it had always been part of the job.
- A person narrows the job without saying so, and later finds the missing part absent.

The damage is that the two sides are now building against different specifications while both
believe they are agreed.

## What it looks like in the record

- **Compare the job at the start of the window with the job at the surface.** Write down what has
  been added and what has gone. That difference is the finding, and every item in it needs an
  anchor.
- **An addition first appearing in the model's own output**, with nothing behind it in any
  request.
- **A requirement appearing in a person's later turn phrased as a reminder** — "and obviously it
  needs to…", "as agreed…", "don't forget the…" — where the record contains no earlier statement
  of it. The phrasing is doing the work of a decision that was never made.
- **A stated deliverable that simply stops being mentioned.**
- **The absence of any message anywhere saying the scope changed.** That absence is part of the
  claim, so search for it and say where you searched.

## Telling it apart

| Easily confused with | The question that separates them |
|---|---|
| `ambiguous-instruction` | About **which thing** or about **how much**? Which → ambiguity. How much → scope. |
| `thread-overload` | Was more work **decided on**, or did unfinished work merely accumulate inside an unchanged job? Decided → scope. Accumulated → overload. |
| `missing-context` | Was the requirement **never supplied**, or supplied **late and backdated** as though it had always been there? Late-and-backdated → scope injection. |
| `stale-constraint` | Is an old rule being applied to a job that changed (stale), or is the job different from the one agreed (scope)? |
| `premature-parallelism` | Was the extra work part of the job but begun too early, or not part of the job at all? |

## What rules it out

- **The change was surfaced and agreed.** Quote the message where it happened. If the agreed
  change caused the failure, your origin is that agreement — a decision that was made, not one
  that was smuggled.
- **The apparent addition sits inside a reasonable reading of the original ask.** Then nothing was
  injected; you may be looking at ambiguity instead.
- **The job did not actually change.** Line up the start and the surface honestly. Work that
  looks different but delivers the same thing is not a scope change.
