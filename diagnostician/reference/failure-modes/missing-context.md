# Missing context

**In one line.** The model needed a fact or a decision that was never put in front of it, so it
supplied one of its own.

## What it is

Not too many readings — **none**. No reading of what was actually in the window yields the
answer, so the model had to invent it in order to continue.

Inventions of this kind are almost always plausible. That is the whole difficulty: a model
filling a gap produces something sensible, in the right shape, consistent with everything around
it. Nothing about it announces itself as a guess. Work then gets built on it, and the gap only
becomes visible when the invented thing collides with the real one.

Where the missing thing actually lived is irrelevant to your finding. It may have been perfectly
clear in the person's head, or written down in a file, or settled in an earlier session. If it
was not on the recorder, it was not in front of the model.

## What it looks like in the record

- **A specific with no source.** A name, a path, a format, a number, a convention or a decision
  appears in the model's output, and no message anywhere earlier in the window supplies it.
  Search every channel before you claim this — including attachments.
- **In the reasoning channel, where you have it:** the model noting that it does not have
  something and proceeding on an assumption. It usually reads as reasonable inference rather than
  as guesswork, which is why it slides past.
- **Surprise, not correction.** When the person finally reacts, the character of the reaction is
  someone seeing something for the first time — not someone spotting a mistake in something they
  recognise.
- **The invention is consistent.** Once made, it is used the same way every time, which makes it
  look established.

## The absence has to be demonstrated

This mode is a claim that something is not there, and a loose absence claim cannot be argued
with, which makes it worthless. Before naming it you must be able to state all three:

1. **What you searched for** — the specific fact or decision.
2. **Where you searched** — the exact message range, and every channel in it.
3. **Where it bit** — the message, quoted, at which the model had to proceed without it.

## Telling it apart

| Easily confused with | The question that separates them |
|---|---|
| `ambiguous-instruction` | Count the candidates. None → missing context. Two or more → the model was choosing, not inventing. |
| `stale-constraint` | Was the information present but out of date? Present-and-old → stale. Never present → missing. |
| `unverified-claim-accepted` | Would supplying a **fact** have prevented it, or would **checking an assertion** have prevented it? Fact → missing context. Check → unverified claim. |
| `scope-injection` | Was a requirement never supplied at all, or supplied late and treated as though it had always been there? Late-and-backdated → scope injection. |

## What rules it out

- **The material is in the window**, in any channel. Then it was not missing, and something else
  went wrong with it.
- **You cannot see the contents of an attachment or a tool result.** If the record lists a file
  as present but your copy does not carry its text, you do not know whether the missing thing was
  in it. That is a gap in *your* recorder, not a finding about the session. Say what you would
  need and either get it or treat the question as open.
